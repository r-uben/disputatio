# Method 1: Structured Disputation

This method provides the **format** for conducting a debate about a single point. It does not by itself find issues — it structures how an issue is examined once found.

## Procedure

1. **Formulate the quaestio.** State the point under examination as a precise yes/no question. Not "the paper's calibration is wrong" but "Is the calibrated value of X consistent with the paper's own definition of X?"

2. **Generate objections.** Produce **independent objections** to the affirmative answer — as many as the issue warrants, each from a genuinely different direction. Independence means no two objections rest on the same core argument.

   For each objection, write:
   - The objection itself as a declarative statement
   - The chain of reasoning that leads to it (not just the conclusion)
   - The specific evidence or passage in the paper that supports it
   - What would force the author to **concede** this objection — i.e., what counter-evidence would make the objection undeniable

3. **Write the sed contra.** State the single strongest counter-argument on the opposing side. This is not the full defense — it is the clearest reason to believe the affirmative answer despite the objections. Often it is a passage from the paper itself that directly addresses the point.

4. **Write the respondeo.** Provide the considered answer to the quaestio. This is the author's best case for the affirmative. It may concede some of the objections' ground while holding the core claim.

5. **Reply to each objection individually.** For every objection raised in step 2, write a specific reply:
   - Either the objection is **conceded** — in which case the respondeo must be revised to acknowledge the concession
   - Or the objection is **answered** — in which case the reply must cite specific evidence or reasoning that defeats it
   - Or the objection **survives** — in which case the respondeo is weakened at that point

6. **The output is not "keep or drop."** It is the list of objections that survived, the list that were conceded, and the final (possibly weakened) form of the respondeo. The claim after this process is more honest than the claim before.

## When to use

Every round of formal debate should use this structure. The prosecution produces the objections (steps 1-2); the defender produces the sed contra and respondeo (steps 3-4) and replies to each objection (step 5); the synthesizer reads the whole thing and writes the final state.

## What it protects against

- **Hand-waving defense.** Every objection must be answered individually. The defender cannot respond with a general counter-argument that ignores the specific objections.
- **Asymmetric attack.** The prosecution must produce multiple independent objections, not one objection restated three ways.
- **False convergence.** The synthesizer sees the full list of replies and can distinguish between "objection conceded but claim still stands" and "objection answered and defeated."
