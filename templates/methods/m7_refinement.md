# Method 7: Iterative Refinement

This is the synthesis method. It operates after each round of attack and defense to produce a refined claim that becomes the input to the next round. It is not used to find new issues — it operates on an existing issue that has been debated.

## Procedure

After a completed round (prosecution + defense), the synthesizer executes the following steps in order.

### Step 1: Write down the original claim

State the claim as it entered the round, with full precision. If this is the first round, the claim is the original issue found in discovery. If this is a later round, the claim is the refined version from the previous round.

### Step 2: Inventory what the attack established

Do not paraphrase the attack. Specifically:
- What did the prosecution **demonstrate** (as opposed to assert)?
- Which evidence did it produce that actually supports its case?
- Which of its claims were backed by specific passages, and which were hand-waved?

The inventory separates the signal from the rhetoric.

### Step 3: Inventory what the defense established

Same procedure for the defense:
- What did the defense **demonstrate**?
- Which of its replies produced specific counter-evidence?
- Which replies were concessions (acknowledging the attack was right but arguing the consequences are bounded)?
- Which replies were actual refutations?

### Step 4: Identify the surviving ground

List what both sides now agree on. This is the **accepted facts** of the debate. It includes:
- Claims the defense conceded
- Evidence the attack produced that the defense did not contest
- Background conditions both sides took for granted

### Step 5: Identify what has been refuted

List components of the original claim that have been killed — parts of the thesis that neither side now defends. This is the **refuted components** list. It may include:
- Assertions the prosecution disproved that the defense did not try to salvage
- Assertions the defense disproved that the prosecution dropped
- Overstatements that both sides agree were too strong

### Step 6: Identify what remains in dispute

List the claims both sides still disagree on after the round. For each, state the core point of disagreement in one sentence. These are the **open disputes**. They are the input to the next round.

### Step 7: Write the refined claim

Construct the strongest version of the original claim that is consistent with the accepted facts and not touched by the refuted components. This is the **current claim** — what we now believe, conditional on what the debate has established.

Rules for the refinement:
- **Do not preserve strength at the cost of honesty.** If the refined claim is weaker than the original, that is correct. A weaker true claim is worth more than a stronger false one.
- **Incorporate hidden assumptions as explicit conditions.** If the attack exposed a hidden lemma, the refined claim must state it.
- **Name the scope.** If the debate revealed the original was applied too broadly, the refined claim must narrow the scope.
- **Preserve what survived.** If the core mechanism survived, do not abandon it just because the periphery was weakened.

### Step 8: Assess materiality

Give the refined claim an impact label:
- **Material**: affects the paper's central results or main interpretation
- **Local**: affects a specific passage or calibration but not the core
- **None**: the debate resolved the original issue; no further work needed

### Step 9: Decide next step

One of:
- **Continue**: the refined claim is materially different from the original and new objections can be raised against it. A new round begins with rotated roles.
- **Converged**: the refined claim is stable — no material change from the previous round, or both sides have agreed on the final form. The debate ends.
- **Split**: the original issue actually contained two or more independent claims that are being conflated. Split them into separate issues, each to enter debate independently.
- **Escalate**: the debate depends on information neither side can verify (external data, hidden derivations). Mark for human review.

### Step 10: Record the round

Append to the issue's history:
- The original claim
- What the attack established
- What the defense established
- The refined claim
- The decision (continue / converged / split / escalate)

## What this method protects against

- **Premature convergence.** Without this procedure, a synthesizer may declare victory for whichever side rhetorically dominated. The step-by-step inventory forces separation of rhetoric from evidence.
- **Moved goalposts.** If the refined claim is materially different from the original, that difference is recorded. No one can pretend the original claim was always the refined version.
- **Drift into mush.** The method produces a concrete refined claim, not a "both sides have a point" summary. If both sides have a point, the refined claim must say exactly which parts of each are preserved.

## Web search supports this method lightly

The synthesizer rarely needs external information. But if either the attack or defense relies on a cited work, the synthesizer can use web search to verify the citation actually says what is claimed.
