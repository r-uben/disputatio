## 8. Adopting and adapting the pattern

The five patterns in §§2–6 and the worked case in §7 describe what the reference implementation does today. This section is for a reader in an adjacent field — legal-brief audit, biotech-protocol review, ML-paper assessment, social-science manuscript triage — deciding whether to fork the architecture and, if so, where to cut. The honest answer is that the patterns are more portable than the reference implementation, and the reference implementation is more brittle than `SKILL.md` makes it look. Both halves of that sentence matter.

### Three layers of adoptability

The system has three concentric layers, each with a different transfer cost.

The **domain-invariant** layer is the architectural patterns themselves. The cross-architecture panel (§2), the Route B adversarial gate on consensus (§3), the strict-blind isolation between generation and evaluation (§4), the archetype-based decomposition of the literature-engagement task (§5), and the auditable disposition trail that surfaces drops (§6) transfer without modification. So does the writing-time discipline that holds the rest of the pipeline together: every candidate finding ships with a verbatim quote from the source document, a falsifier sentence describing the evidence that would force its withdrawal, and an explicit `direct_quote | derived_inference` tag. The merge step is mechanical bookkeeping, not voting; the panel preserves the structure of agreement rather than flattening it. These are not economics-specific moves — they are responses to two failure modes (correlated errors across training distributions; comparison-target leakage through orchestrator context) that any multi-agent review system inherits.

The **domain-tunable** layer is the components with an obvious analogue in the new field that need re-parameterising. The attack-surface typology in `templates/holistic.md` (theory / empirics / identification / framing / robustness / exposition) is economics-theory-paper-shaped; a legal-brief reviewer probably needs precedent-alignment, jurisdictional-scope, and procedural-posture axes, a biotech-protocol reviewer dosing, control-arm matching, and assay validation. The discovery method bank inherits the constraint: M0 close-reading and M2 contradiction-finding generalise as written, but M3 transformations, M6 causal disentangling, and M8 derivation tracing all assume a paper with theorems and equations to walk; a field whose load-bearing artifact is a clinical protocol or a contract clause needs an M8 analogue that walks dosing schedules or contractual obligations. The literature-engagement backend swaps cleanly (PubMed, SSRN, arXiv, Westlaw — Semantic Scholar's unified graph covers a surprisingly broad slice), but the suppress-canonical list needs full replacement: every field has its foundational representatives that a language model returns by default unless steered off. The model-family routing reflects what we have observed on theory-economics papers; it is a starting hypothesis, not a finding.

The **domain-specific** layer is what must be rewritten outright. The five reasoning archetypes from §5 were derived from one referee's report on one paper. The *taxonomy structure* (decompose the librarian task along the question-shape axis, not the topic axis) generalises; the *specific archetype mix* almost certainly does not. Legal review will lean on "same-instrument-different-domain" as precedent translated across jurisdictions; biotech on "mechanism-isomorphic-predecessor" in an adjacent organism; ML on "substitution-of-assumption" as relaxing an architectural commitment. A forker who copies the five verbatim inherits a Zhang-shaped vocabulary applied to a not-Zhang-shaped paper. The category vocabulary for findings needs the same treatment, as does the seven-mode shared-hallucination checklist Route B's defender walks: the *checklist mechanism* transfers, but a legal-review fork will probably need a "precedent miscitation" mode and a biotech fork a "dosage transcription error" mode. Dropping modes the field never produces is as important as adding the ones it does.

### Currently brittle parts of the reference implementation

A forker who reads `SKILL.md` end-to-end will encounter machinery that is documented but not running. This is the part of the section that earns the section's premise.

`SKILL.md` defines four post-discovery audit phases — Phase 1.5 (obligation extraction), 2.5 (claim-validity), 2.6 (scope-framing), 2.7 (expositional craft) — each with its own per-family triage, integrator, and dedicated calibrator (Phase 3g, 3v, 3s, 3e). All four are gated by per-track flags and skipped by default; they have not, at the time of writing, been observed in a complete end-to-end run on the same paper. Treat them as designs rather than measurements. The benchmark that would make any of this falsifiable does not exist either: Issue #19 (three to five sealed-report papers under a protocol frozen before reading any of them) is the right validation path and is not built. A forker faces the same n=1 problem the reference implementation does; the strict-blind discipline of §4 does not retroactively manufacture a corpus.

The `/chrome` MCP backend driving the A3 Scholar fill-in is rate-limit-prone in practice; a fork should default straight to the Semantic Scholar API (Issue #48 is the in-flight replacement). The cost of getting this wrong is silent degradation — throttled queries fall back to canonical-syllabus papers, and A3's output collapses toward v1's training-memory-only behaviour. The concern-axes typology in `templates/holistic.md` is also hardcoded. Issue #49 (generate concern-axes per paper rather than hardcoding them) would address the three documented Zhang blind spots — structural-primitives questions, empirical-anchoring-for-theory questions, formal-apparatus-internal-consistency questions — none of which the hardcoded typology has an axis for. Without it closed, a fork inherits the same blind-spot structure: whatever the hand-written typology omits, the discovery tracks systematically under-detect.

### Where to start your fork

The shortest path is: read `SKILL.md` end to end; then read the discovery shape (`templates/orient.md`, `templates/holistic.md`, `templates/discover_holistic.md`, `templates/discover_broad.md`, `templates/discover_narrow.md`) and the consensus-mode section of `templates/synthesize.md` for Route B from the synthesizer's side; then read the 2026-05-20 strict-blind dev log to see what evaluation contamination looks like in practice and why the fix is architectural rather than procedural. The fifth step is the one that matters most: fork on a small scale first. One paper, one model family per role, no calibration, no Route B, no literature-engagement track. Get the orientation-to-panel flow producing structured findings with verbatim quotes; then add calibration; then add Route B once enough consensus findings exist for the gate to fire meaningfully; then add the literature-engagement track. Each layer is independently runnable. Building all of it at once is how the architecture becomes incomprehensible, and is how the blind-discipline rules get re-violated at every layer simultaneously.

The three-family floor (Anthropic / OpenAI / Google) is the smallest configuration we have measured. Adding a fourth family — Kimi, an Ollama-served open-weights model — is laid out in `docs/adding-agents.md`, with cross-family count at full weight and cross-instance-of-same-family at half weight. Whether four families meaningfully improves the consensus floor is unmeasured. Running below three is supported as a documented reduced mode or a separately-named `solo-draft` command, precisely to keep the cross-architecture invariant visible on the product surface.

### Failure modes a forker should expect

Four failure modes will show up regardless of how carefully the fork is built. Evaluation contamination at the orchestrator-context level is the failure §4 walks through; the diagnostic is that the orchestrator session has at some point read both the pipeline state and the evaluation target, and the symptom that reveals it is a recall number that looks suspiciously round. The fix is structural — subagent dispatch with explicit read-allowlists, comparison artifacts written to a separate `_evaluation/` directory the pipeline phases cannot read. Audit the session graph, not the prompts. Shared hallucination at the multi-family-consensus level is the failure §3 addresses; the diagnostic is the combination of maximum severity and unanimous family support, and the fix is the Route B red-team challenge — a forker without it will ship the three findings from §3's Han-Hu-Zhang example as the strongest items in the report. Calibration overconfidence is the failure where the annotator agrees with the discovery agent because both were trained on similar corpora and pattern-match the same way; the reference implementation's mitigation is an upgraded re-annotator on flagged rows (codex `gpt-5.4-mini` on Pass 1, codex `gpt-5.4` full on the polish re-annotation), and on a fork the fix is rotating the re-annotator to a different family from Pass 1 wherever possible. Pre-publication confidentiality leakage via external API calls is specific to systems reviewing unpublished work; the literature-engagement track's Scholar and Semantic Scholar calls are the highest-risk surface, and the reference implementation's mitigation is the discipline that A3 queries derive from archetype-question keyword stems and specific-paper-title fragments only, never from verbatim sentences of the unpublished manuscript.

### Do not fork this if

The architecture is not the right answer for every review domain. If the domain has structured, fully machine-readable evaluation criteria — regulatory compliance against a documented checklist, automated style-guide enforcement, conformance to a fixed schema — a single-LLM pipeline or a rule-based system is simpler and probably better. The cross-architecture panel buys precision against ambiguous interpretive judgments; it buys very little against a checklist that already has a deterministic answer. If the domain has no notion of specialised-comparator literature, because the review is of a purely empirical artifact with no theoretical lineage, the literature-engagement track is dead weight: the librarian gap is a real failure mode in academic review specifically because senior referees name comparator papers in the first paragraph of their report, and a domain where that move does not exist does not need a separate phase to address its absence. If the use case is high-throughput, low-stakes review — triaging thousands of submissions at editorial screen rather than auditing a single paper carefully — the cross-architecture panel is the wrong precision/cost trade-off, and a single LLM with a self-critic loop is probably the better fit. The pattern is for cases where false confidence is materially costly. When it is not, simpler systems win.

What this section gives a forker is therefore not a turnkey reference implementation but a map of where the pattern is solid, where it needs re-tuning, and where the spec runs ahead of what has been built. The disposition trail and the Route B audit trail are real architectural contributions that survive transfer; the five-archetype taxonomy and the seven-mode shared-hallucination checklist are starting points calibrated against one paper in one field, and a serious fork should expect to rebuild both. An adopter inherits an n=1 case study, four documented systematic blind spots, a benchmark that does not exist, and a set of architectural patterns whose value comes from making review structure auditable rather than making review output automatically correct. That is the pattern worth lifting. The reference implementation is one instance of it; better instances are the point.

I have not foregrounded these in the body, but they are the obvious limitations of the prescription if §9 picks them up.
- The section asserts that the four audit phases (1.5 / 2.5 / 2.6 / 2.7) and their dedicated calibrators (3g / 3v / 3s / 3e) have "not been observed in a complete end-to-end run on the same paper at the time of writing." - The "do not fork this if" paragraph names three exclusion conditions as declarative claims because the brief required them as non-negotiable content. The third (high-throughput low-stakes → single-LLM-with-self-critic-loop wins) is the most assertion-heavy and is not backed by any measurement; it follows from the precision/cost trade-off argument but could be challenged.
