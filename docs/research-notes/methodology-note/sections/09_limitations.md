## 9. Limitations and failure modes

The note describes an architectural pattern, not a validated performance claim. Section 1 set that framing — "the interesting contribution is the pattern, not the number" — and §§2–8 honour it section by section. This section consolidates those qualifications and adds the failure-modes catalogue a forker needs at hand. This is not a list of warnings about the architecture; it is a list of gaps in what we have measured.

### Claim-level limitations

**n=1 prospective case study.** Every empirical number in this note traces to a single paper. The seven-of-nine strict-blind literature recall (§5), the three-of-four Route B catch (§3), and the 80→34→25+9 disposition-trail walkthrough (§6) are observations on the Han-Hu-Zhang run, not summaries across a corpus. The strict-blind discipline of §4 guarantees the execution was clean; it does not manufacture a second paper. The benchmark that would close this — Issue #19, three to five sealed-report papers under a protocol frozen before any of them is read — is the right validation path and is not built. Every number in this note is evidence about how the architecture behaved on one paper, not a prospective claim against an unseen target.

**Design overfit on the archetype taxonomy.** Section 5 makes this explicit at the point the empirical recall is reported: the five reasoning archetypes were derived by reading Ref #2's exact phrasing for his named references on one paper. Zhang is therefore partly a design case for this taxonomy, not a fully held-out test. The strict-blind dispatch of A1, A2, and A3 keeps the execution Ref-#2-naive but does not retroactively make the *design* Ref-#2-naive. The seven-of-nine recall is consistent with both "the archetypes track what senior referees do" and "the archetypes track what *this* referee did on *this* paper"; a second referee on the same paper, or the same referee on a different paper, would carry a different question-shape vocabulary, and neither has been collected.

**Three substantive concern types the holistic pass cannot generate.** The Han-Hu-Zhang case (§7) surfaces three categories of concern Ref #2 raised that disputatio's hardcoded attack-surface typology has no axes for: structural-primitives questions about parameters that ought to be endogenous (κ_i and α_i in the Zhang model), suggestions for back-of-envelope numerical exercises in a theory paper, and Q-measure-heterogeneity / absence-of-arbitrage concerns at the contract-price level. The structural fix is Issue #49 — generate concern-axes per paper instead of hardcoding theory / empirics / identification / framing / robustness / exposition. Until that ships, every fork inherits the same blind-spot structure: whatever the hand-written typology omits, the discovery tracks systematically under-detect.

**Calibration relies on a within-family upgraded re-annotator.** The reference implementation runs codex `gpt-5.4-mini` on Pass 1 and codex `gpt-5.4` full on the polish re-annotation. The two annotators share training corpora and architectural priors; when Pass 1 agrees with the discovery agent because both pattern-match the same way, Pass 2 can inherit the same pattern-match. The mitigation flagged in §8 — rotate the re-annotator to a different family from Pass 1 — has not been measured. The 0% post-calibration overclaim rate in the disposition trail is an internal quality-gate number, not external validation; domain-expert adjudication is the missing measurement.

**Single-domain validation.** Everything we have observed is on theory economics papers. Section 8's three-layer adoptability model — invariant, tunable, specific — is derived from imagining how legal review, biotech-protocol audit, ML-paper assessment, and social-science manuscript triage would each cut the architecture, not from a fork that has actually been built in any of those fields. A serious cross-domain claim requires a forked implementation on at least one non-economics paper with its own sealed referee benchmark. We do not have one. The portability paragraphs closing each pattern section are hypotheses about transfer cost, not measurements of it.

### Failure modes catalogue

The four failure modes already named in §8 are restated here so a forker can find them in one place; each carries a pointer to where the note treats it in depth.

- **Evaluation contamination at the orchestrator-context level.** The session graph, not the prompts, is the surface that leaks; the orchestrator's choice of which queries to dispatch carried comparison-target knowledge into our own first run. §4 is the worked example.
- **Shared hallucination at the multi-family-consensus level.** Three independent architectures can pattern-match the same wrong reading of the same passage. §3 is the worked example, Route B the mitigation.
- **Calibration overconfidence.** Within-family re-annotation is not architecturally distinct from Pass 1; cross-family rotation is the unmeasured fix. §8's failure-modes paragraph.
- **Pre-publication confidentiality leakage.** External literature-search calls on unpublished work are a confidentiality surface even when queries derive from keyword stems. A fork reviewing unpublished work must audit this surface explicitly. §8's failure-modes paragraph.

Four further failure modes have emerged from continued operation of the architecture beyond the Zhang run. They sit at the same altitude — architectural failure modes, not new thesis claims — and the note has not yet treated them in depth.

**Silent infrastructure-failure drops.** A polish-rewrite or re-annotation ticket can fail to produce output for reasons unrelated to the substance of a finding: CLI crash, empty model response, OAuth expiry mid-run, model-capacity 429. After the retry budget is exhausted the candidate is dropped, and the drop reason is logged honestly — but in the published panel the result is indistinguishable from a substantively-killed finding. A reader sees "dropped at Pass 2" without knowing whether annotator judgment or infrastructure killed the row. The architectural fix is to widen the drop-reason vocabulary so infrastructure failures are tagged separately, and to add a one-tier-demote fallback rather than a hard drop when the polish step itself fails.

**Route B kernel-loss when a broad consensus claim is broken.** The merge-time atomicity rule ideally splits a broad finding into atomic siblings, but the broadest framing of a real concern can still be the row Route B's red-team operates on. When the defender breaks that framing — by locating verbatim text the consensus missed, in the §3 sense — the row drops entirely, even if a narrower kernel would survive scrutiny. The narrower kernel only ships if it was independently emitted as a separate row at merge time; the synthesizer can ship or drop, but cannot *narrow*. The structural fix is a third Route B verdict — `consensus_narrowed` — that preserves a specifically-named survivor sub-claim rather than killing the row.

**Unequal effective independence across the three families.** The panel of §2 treats three-family consensus as a uniform precision signal. In practice the three families have very different effective coverage on a given paper: one may produce twice the candidates of another, one may flag almost everything at the same priority tier (low discrimination), one may stay lean enough that its agreement is closer to concurrence than to an independent third read. A Route B fire resting on one family's depth plus two families' concurrence is a weaker independence signal than the architecture's framing suggests. The mitigation is to track per-family discrimination metrics — priority spread, coverage of the attack-surface index, candidate density — and surface them in the panel render, so a reader can see whether a consensus was earned independently or absorbed by inheritance.

**LLM-coding methodology in target papers is a class the architecture cannot yet audit at the right altitude.** When a target paper uses its own LLM pipeline to produce a structural empirical input — increasingly common in economics and political science — disputatio surfaces the pipeline as an attack surface and produces verbal critiques (prompt sensitivity not documented; aggregation rules not stress-tested). What it does not yet do is propose the principled fix: replace the LLM-as-direct-measurement step with a transparent econometric recovery, treating the model's ordinal labels as noisy observations and fitting a graded-response or monotone-latent-index model on top. The system is one architectural step behind on the class of papers it most needs to handle, and the gap is not a prompt or coverage issue — there is no "here is the alternative architecture" move in its current repertoire. The fix is a sixth pattern in this note, written after a fork has actually built and run such a recovery model on a real paper.

### What this note supports, and what it does not

A reader may reasonably take from this note a set of architectural patterns — the cross-architecture finding panel, the Route B red-team on consensus, the strict-blind phase isolation, the archetype-driven literature engagement, the auditable disposition trail — together with the structural reasons each exists, a guide to forking them for an adjacent domain, and the failure modes those patterns address (and so far do not).

The reader may not take from this note a validated recall or precision claim, a benchmark across multiple papers or multiple fields, a measurement of the calibration-overconfidence fix, or a demonstration that the architecture works outside theory economics. None of that is in the note because none of it is in the data we have. Closing those gaps is not a matter of better prompting; it is a matter of running the system on more papers, in more fields, against more sealed benchmarks, with the protocol frozen before any of them is read. Better measurements are the point.
